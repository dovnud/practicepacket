# 7. Affiliates and other revenue

Date: 14 November 2026. The Amazon tag in the brief would be `domainname-20`. Nothing was signed up for. Rates are US unless noted.

## Amazon Associates

Source: Amazon.com Associates Program Standard Commission Income Statement, help topic `GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ`, fetched 14 November 2026. The page does not show an effective date. Rates can change on short notice.

Fixed standard rates that matter for this site:

| Category | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Physical books, kitchen, automotive | 4.50% |
| Apparel, shoes, luggage, and the listed Amazon devices (Kindle hardware, Fire tablet, Echo, Fire TV) | 4.00% |
| All other categories (the fallback) | 4.00% |
| Toys, furniture, home, home improvement, lawn and garden, pets, headphones, beauty, musical instruments, business and industrial, outdoors, tools, sports, baby products | 3.00% |
| PC and PC components, DVD and Blu-ray | 2.50% |
| Grocery, health and personal care, Amazon Fresh, physical video games | 1.00% |
| Gift cards | 0.00% |

Office products are not a named row. A hole punch or a ream of paper falls in “all other” at 4% only if Amazon does not file it under home, business and industrial, or another 3% row. Check the category on the product page before promising 4%.

Special bounties on the same page, relevant ones only: Audible Standard free trial or paid monthly membership $20; Audible Premium Plus annual $25; Kindle Unlimited free trial $3; Amazon Kids+ free trial $3; Amazon Business registration $15; Prime free trial or paid membership $12. Bounties have eligibility rules and are easy to lose. Do not build the plan on them.

A secondary write-up (amawp.com, checked against the fee schedule in August 2026, as that page states) says that from 14 April 2026, purchases that arrive through a paid or boosted ad linking to Amazon are excluded, and that an order must ship and be paid within 180 days. The operating-agreement change was not re-read on Amazon’s “what’s changed” page. Treat the April 2026 exclusion as secondary, and do not run paid ads to Amazon links until that page is read.

Practical ceiling: a parent who buys a $15 workbook at 4.5% is about $0.68. A $20 toy at 3% is $0.60. Affiliates of this kind are a side line next to ads or a membership. They fit a short “books we print from” note on a skill page, not a buying guide.

## Teachers Pay Teachers

Two different ideas, often mixed.

**Selling on TPT** is not an affiliate program. Help article “Seller Fees and Payout Rates”, updated 1 October 2026:

| | Basic Seller | Exclusive Seller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fee | $29 once, non-refundable | $59.95 per year |
| Payout | 55% of sales | 77.5% of sales. The article says Exclusive sellers earn 80% until 1 January 2027, then 77.5%. |
| Transaction fee | $0.30 per resource | $0.15 per resource, only on orders under $3 |
| Exclusivity | No | Digital education resources must be sold only on TPT |

Premium Seller is closed to new sign-ups as of 1 October 2026. Exclusive sellers may give resources away free on and off TPT, and may not charge on TPT for something they give away elsewhere. A free worksheet site plus an Exclusive TPT store selling the same PDFs conflicts with that rule. Basic Seller does not have the exclusivity line. Selling a bundle on TPT as a Basic seller, and giving a smaller set away on the site, is the shape that matches the written rules. Read the terms again before uploading. The $29 fee is real money; this research did not spend it.

**Referring TPT buyers** for a commission: a public rate was not found.

**IXL family program for TPT sellers.** TPT’s seller blog describes 15% on sales referred to IXL, Rosetta Stone, Wyzant, ABCya, and Carson Dellosa, for Teacher-Authors in good standing. The example on that page: Rosetta Stone average order $179, commission about $26.85, which is 15% of 179. That program is for TPT sellers, and the blog post is undated in the extract. Confirm before relying on it.

## What is worth doing in phase 1

- Amazon books at 4.50% and toys at 3.00%, a few titles per skill, only where a physical book truly extends the sheet. Tag form `domainname-20` after the domain exists.
- One software offer once the site has a real audience. Outschool’s “up to 20%” is the cleanest public rate found. IXL at 15% is the cleanest flat rate found, and it may require the TPT-seller path.
- Do not cover the page in offers. The print page is the product. Incumbent ads.txt files show these sites make money from ads and memberships, not from a wall of affiliate buttons.
- A paid PDF bundle at a K5-like price ($24 a year, or a one-time grade bundle) is a hypothesis, not a rate. File 06 shows why one sale beats a thousand cheap ad visits. It also adds support email. The brief says the public address is `contact@<domain>` only. A paid tier needs a way to receive those messages without putting a personal name on the site. A shared inbox under that address is enough.
