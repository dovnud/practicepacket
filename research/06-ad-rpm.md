# 6. Ad RPM and network thresholds

Date: 14 November 2026. The owner’s working range is $8–20 per 1,000 visits. This file tries to confirm or refute that. It does not replace it with a made-up worksheet RPM.

## Thresholds, from the networks

Verified by fetching the pages on 14 November 2026.

**Journey by Mediavine.** Help article “Journey Minimum Requirements”: at least 1,000 premium sessions in a 30-day period, GA4 connected, original brand-safe content, an engaged audience, content updated often. Automatic disqualifiers include an incompatible CMS (Wix and Blogspot are the examples), extreme brand-safety problems, excessive use of AI, and being under 1,000 premium sessions. The article does not define “premium” in the fetched text. A secondary page (pubsleuth.com, “read 10 November 2026”) says Journey counts US, UK, Canada, and Australia sessions toward the 1,000. That country list is secondary, not on the Journey article itself.

**Mediavine, full.** Help page “What does it take to get approved by Mediavine?”, copyright line 2026: Journey needs over 1,000 sessions; Mediavine Official needs $5,000+ in annual ad revenue. Also: original content, human traffic, good standing with AdSense and Ad Exchange. The old “50,000 sessions” line is not on this page. Sites that still quote 50,000 sessions are out of date.

**Raptive.** Help article “Who is eligible for Raptive?”, bylined Jasmine, 26 September 2026: minimum 25,000 pageviews a month. From 25,000 to 99,999 pageviews: 50% of traffic from the US, UK, Canada, New Zealand, and/or Australia; the site must be easy to put ads on; long-form content on the majority of pages; content original with meaningful human involvement; GA4; domain at least 6 months old. At 100,000 pageviews and above: the country share drops to 40%, and the fetched text does not repeat the long-form line. Human involvement stays. Raptive’s 16 October 2025 blog post says the old bar was 100,000 pageviews and that the Rise pilot (50,000–100,000) was folded into Insider.

Worksheet pages fail the under-100,000 long-form rule on a plain reading. A new site should not plan on Raptive at 25,000 pageviews unless the HTML pages are genuinely articles. At 100,000 pageviews the long-form line is absent from that section, and “meaningful human involvement” is still there. A purely generated library can fail that sentence.

**Playwire.** Common Core Sheets uses it (file 01). A current Playwire minimum was not fetched from Playwire. Not found.

**Ezoic.** A secondary roundup (outrank.so) says new sites since February 2026 need 250,000 monthly active users, with a smaller incubator. Not confirmed on Ezoic’s own page in this pass. Not used for the decision.

## The $8–20 per 1,000 visits assumption

Units first. Mediavine and Raptive publishers talk about session RPM and about page RPM, and the two are not the same. Productive Blogging’s 2026 survey defines RPM as earnings per 1,000 pageviews. The owner’s range is per 1,000 visits. A visit with one page is the same number. A visit with five pages is five times the page RPM.

**No worksheet-site RPM was found.** K5, Math-Drills, Super Teacher, and Superstar do not publish income. Mediavine and Raptive do not publish an education-niche RPM. whatisrpm.com (verified 1 August 2026, as that page states) says Mediavine’s public posts are seasonal commentary, not a niche table, and that AdSense does not publish RPM benchmarks.

**Survey evidence, all niches, pageviews.**

Productive Blogging, “How much do bloggers REALLY earn in 2026?”, updated 28 May 2026. 129 responses, 2 January–29 April 2026. Self-selected readers of that site. The author says food, lifestyle, and travel were the largest niche sets, and that niche samples are too small to rank niches. In the prose, not only in a chart:

- Ads as the main revenue stream: average RPM $33.16 per 1,000 pageviews, versus $33.80 in the prior survey.
- Journey by Mediavine: $28.60 this survey, versus $11.15 last year.
- Raptive’s RPM is $0.49 higher than Mediavine’s. The absolute 2026 Mediavine and Raptive chart values were not in the article text. A secondary page (pubsleuth.com) transcribes them as $42.43 and $41.94. Those two absolutes are not independently verified here.
- Digital products as the main stream: $535.90 per 1,000 pageviews. That number is why a $24/year membership can beat ads at small scale, and why it is a different product.

The 2025 survey figures quoted by outrank.so (187 bloggers): Raptive $34.86, Mediavine $31.55, Journey $11.15, Ezoic $11.08, AdSense $2.34, per 1,000 pageviews. The 2026 article confirms the Journey comparison ($11.15 then, $28.60 now) and that 2025 Raptive was “over $3 higher” than Mediavine, which matches $34.86 minus $31.55. The 2025 AdSense figure is secondary.

**What $8–20 per 1,000 visits corresponds to.**

K5’s Semrush pages/visit is 8.65 and Similarweb’s is 8.64. At AdSense-like page RPMs, that depth lands inside or just above $8–20 per 1,000 visits. At Journey or Mediavine page RPMs from the blogger survey, the same depth is far above $20. The owner’s range is a reasonable AdSense planning band for a multi-page session. It is not supported as a Mediavine or Raptive band. It is too high for a one-page PDF download on AdSense.

Third-party calculators (revenuebyadapp.com, “updated October 2026”) estimate an education site at about $720 a month on Mediavine at 50,000 sessions and two pages per session, and show a tier-1 page RPM of $15.30 using their own multiplier. The page says the figures are their model, not a network quote. Not used as a verified RPM. Their education seasonality index (September high, July low) is the same kind of model.

Parenting ranges of “$5–$15 RPM” on earnifyhub.com and similar 2026 blog posts do not name a sample. Not used.

## Will a worksheet site even be accepted?

- Superstar Worksheets is on Mediavine, with Semrush at 1.34M October visits. Acceptance has happened for at least one printable site. Their page length and human-editing process were not reviewed.
- K5 and Math-Drills, the two clearest drill sites, are on a single AdSense line. That is consistent with AdSense being enough, or with premium networks having said no, or with a choice to keep the pages clean. ads.txt cannot tell those apart.
- Journey’s “excessive use of AI” line is the practical risk for a generated library. Code-generated arithmetic with a human-written teaching note is a different artifact from a page of model prose. The network decides. Do not assume Journey at 1,000 sessions.
- Raptive’s long-form rule blocks the obvious plan until pageviews are high, and maybe after that too.
- Ads on a thin page also cost speed and trust. Dad’s Worksheets blocks PDFs in robots and sells the HTML page. That is the inventory. A direct-to-PDF site has little to show an ad on.

## Planning numbers to use until the site has its own

Label: ESTIMATED, for a cash plan only.

- Months 1–12, if traffic is small and the network is AdSense: $8–20 per 1,000 visits only if people view several HTML pages. Use $5 per 1,000 visits if the product is a single download. The bottom of the owner’s range is the honest AdSense case, not the middle.
- After a premium network, if one will have the site: do not book $8–20. Book a wide band of $15–$40 per 1,000 pageviews (survey country, not this niche) and multiply by measured pages per visit. Revisit when the first invoice exists.
- Membership at the incumbent price ($23.95–$24.95 per year, file 01) is worth more than ads until visits are large. At $20 per 1,000 visits, one $24 membership equals about 1,200 ad-supported visits. That is why K5 and Super Teacher charge so little and still bother.
