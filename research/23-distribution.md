# 23. Links and distribution

Date: 14 November 2026. A percentage of traffic from Pinterest was not on any public page we could read. Where a source names Pinterest without a number, the number is not invented.

## What is public

Similarweb’s October 2026 page for K5: organic search is 64.74% of desktop visits. Direct is second. Organic social is third. The social networks are not broken out with shares on that capture. Affiliate is called the most underused channel.

Similarweb’s September 2026 page for Super Teacher: organic search 54.39% of desktop. Direct second. Mail third. Social, as a sentence and not a percent: YouTube, then Pinterest, then Instagram. Seven social networks. No Pinterest percentage.

Semrush, 11 November 2026, Super Teacher desktop journey: direct 44.09%, then google.com 12.84%. After the visit, people go to Google and to Teachers Pay Teachers. That is a path, not a Pinterest stat.

K5’s FAQ forbids posting their worksheets on a publicly accessible website. A new site that copies their files to earn a link will get a takedown, not a link.

## Levers, and what is realistic

**Pinterest.** Teachers pin worksheets. The only evidence in hand is that Pinterest is Super Teacher’s second social source, behind YouTube, with the share hidden. Organic social is already behind search and direct at K5. A pin of the preview image is cheap and worth doing once a page exists. It is not a plan that replaces rankings. A new account has no distribution. Expect a trickle, and do not forecast visits.

**Teacher blogs.** No count of worksheet-site backlinks from teacher blogs was pulled in this pass beyond the referring-domain totals in file 01 (K5 17.41K, Math-Drills 12.45K, Super Teacher 14.23K, Common Core Sheets not in that Semrush set). Those domains were earned over years. A new site asking for links in month one will get almost none. Realistic: one genuine note to a blog that already links to K5 or Math-Drills for a skill the new page does better, after the page is live. Not a campaign of fifty.

**School and district resource pages.** The Virginia teachers thread (7 August 2023) points to the state DOE’s old tests, a paid SOL site, Super Teacher, and TPT. Nobody there is waiting to add an unknown domain to a district list. District pages also move slowly and often nofollow resource lists. Not a first-year lever.

**TPT free listings.** The elementary-teacher thread (19–20 December 2024) says people filter TPT for free. File 07: a Basic seller pays $29 once and keeps 55%, and free items must stay the minority of a store. An Exclusive seller may not sell the same digital file elsewhere. A free TPT listing can point at the site. It also puts the PDF on TPT’s domain, which already has the authority (Semrush Authority Score 77). That can leak the demand to TPT instead of building the site. Realistic only as a thin teaser that links home, under a Basic account, after reading the current seller rules. Do not upload the 200-sheet library there.

**Facebook teacher groups.** A Canadian teachers thread (April 2022) mentions an Ontario Google Drive group. That is one anecdote, and the group is for Ontario teachers sharing files, not a growth channel with a measured click rate. Groups often ban self-promotion. Posting links there is a good way to get removed. Skip it as a strategy.

**The realistic set.** Search, then direct. The pages have to rank or be remembered. YouTube is the social source Super Teacher actually leads with, and a new channel will not, in this test. A single short video per skill is a later experiment, not part of the 50 URLs. Email is how K5 and Super Teacher keep direct visits. A new site has no list. Do not buy one.
