# 8. Standards, copyright, and names

Date: 14 November 2026. This is not legal advice. It is what the public licenses say, and what a worksheet site should not do.

## Common Core

The standards are copyrighted. They are also offered under a public license.

Pages: `https://www.thecorestandards.org/public-license/` and `/terms-of-use/`. The license page returned HTTP 511 to one fetch from this environment. The text below is from the search capture of that page on 14 November 2026, which reproduced the license clauses. Re-open the page before publishing a footer.

License grant, as captured: NGA Center and CCSSO grant a limited, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to copy, publish, distribute, and display the Common Core State Standards for purposes that support the Common Core State Standards Initiative. The standards may be used whole or in excerpts.

Attribution, as captured: acknowledge NGA Center/CCSSO as the sole owners and developers, and make no claim to the contrary. Any publication or public display includes:

> © Copyright 2010. National Governors Association Center for Best Practices and Council of Chief State School Officers. All rights reserved.

States and territories that adopted the standards in whole are exempt from that notice provision. A private website is not a state.

What is outside the license, as captured: the license does not cover the examples. Some examples are third-party material (the captured text names Penguin and McGraw-Hill). Do not copy sample problems, passages, or items from the standards document.

The grant is “for purposes that support” the initiative. It does not say “including commercial worksheet sites” and it does not say “commercial use is forbidden.” That is a gray sentence. Thousands of sites already print the codes. Printing the code and a short skill name is the low-risk end. Pasting every standard’s full text onto a commercial page is the end that depends on that gray sentence. Prefer: show the code (for example 2.MD.C.7), describe the skill in original words, link to the official standard, and put the copyright notice in the footer of any page that actually quotes the standard’s language.

Terms of use, as captured: trademarks, service marks, and logos on the standards site belong to NGA Center, CCSSO, or the party that supplied them. Unauthorized use is described as unlawful. A USPTO registration number for “Common Core” was not retrieved in this pass. Not found. Do not put Common Core in the site name. commoncoresheets.com already exists. “Aligned to 2.NBT.B.5” is a factual reference. “Common Core Worksheets” as a brand is how a trademark argument starts.

The terms also say the standards website may not be used for a commercial purpose without written consent, including co-branding or framing that site. That clause is about their website, not about citing a code. Do not iframe thecorestandards.org.

## Texas TEKS

Texas Education Agency site policies, fetched in search on 14 November 2026 (`tea.texas.gov` site policies):

All content on the TEA site is copyrighted by the Texas Education Agency and cannot be used without written permission, except:

1. Texas public school districts, charter schools, and education service centers may copy materials for district and school educational use.
2. Residents of Texas may copy materials for personal use.

The same page says not to alter or make partial copies, and not to charge except for the cost of reproduction. A person in Texas who is not a district employee needs written approval and possibly a fee to copy materials. Contact on that page: Copyrights@tea.texas.gov.

A national commercial site is outside both exceptions. Do not paste TEKS text. A code such as 3.4(A), plus an original sentence about the skill, plus a link to TEA, is the approach that stays off that page’s prohibitions. Confirm with a lawyer before quoting even a sentence of TEKS on a paid product.

Texas Education Code chapter 31, as captured, is about state-owned open educational materials and licensing of those materials. It is not a grant to copy the TEKS. Do not treat “open education resource” in that chapter as permission to republish the standards.

## Virginia SOL

A Virginia science curriculum-framework PDF (2018 standards, copyright 2019, Virginia Department of Education), as captured: “Reproduction of these materials for instructional purposes in public school classrooms in Virginia is permitted.”

That sentence is a classroom permission for Virginia public schools. It is not a license for a commercial site outside those classrooms. Same rule as TEKS: cite the code, link to the Virginia Department of Education, do not paste the standard.

## NGSS

`nextgenscience.org` trademarks and copyright page, as captured:

- States, districts, schools, teachers, and nonprofit education entities may copy, alter, and rearrange the NGSS without permission.
- They may not represent that their standards are the NGSS unless they adopted all of the performance expectations.
- Trademarks, including the words “Next Generation Science Standards” and the logo, moved from Achieve to WestEd in May 2020. The copyright in the standards is held by the National Academies Press, as that page states.
- Citation the page asks for: NGSS Lead States. 2013. *Next Generation Science Standards: For States, By States.* Washington, DC: The National Academies Press. For the web version, add the section title if not quoting the whole.

A for-profit worksheet site is not in the list “states, districts, schools, teachers, and nonprofit education entities.” Do not assume the nonprofit/school permission covers this project. Science is phase 3 anyway. When it starts, use the performance-expectation code and original wording, and ask before quoting NGSS text. Do not use the NGSS logo.

## Other states

Florida B.E.S.T., New York Next Generation Learning Standards, and California’s framework were not read. Not found in this pass. The safe default is the same: codes and links, original skill language, no state text, no state seals.

## Worksheets themselves

The problems, instructions, and answer keys need to be original. Copying a Math-Drills or K5 layout of the same fact family is how those sites, and the clone wave, will send a complaint. Facts (3+4=7) are not copyrightable. A particular arrangement, artwork, and wording can be.

Generated clock faces and base-ten drawings should be drawn by the site’s own code, not traced from a competitor’s PDF.

“Common Core” in a URL slug (`/common-core/grade-2/`) is a magnet for the trademark question and for every other site using that slug. Put the code on the page. Keep the URL as the skill (file 10).
